Safety alert · TGA22 Sep 2026

Andrographis removed from permitted ingredients over allergy risk

From 17 September 2026 the TGA no longer allows andrographis in new listed medicines, citing reports of anaphylaxis, including a fatal case in June 2024. Companies have until mid-October to voluntarily recall or delist products containing it; after that the TGA says it will take regulatory action. BioCeuticals, which makes ArmaForce, has said it has stopped producing stock for supply, and BrightWell has taken ArmaForce off sale. If you have an andrographis product at home, talk to your pharmacist before taking it; stop and seek medical advice if you notice allergy symptoms, and call 000 for a severe reaction.

Source: TGA ↗ Source: ABC News ↗
TGA · LabelsFrom 1 Oct 2026

New medicine label rules take effect

The old labelling orders (TGO 91 for prescription and TGO 92 for non-prescription medicines) expired on 1 October. The new requirements include declaring allergens such as marine molluscs and sulphites, and spelling out "microgram" instead of "µg" to reduce misreading. Sponsors have up to five years to comply, so you will see both old and new label formats while stock is replaced.

Source: Australian Pharmacist ↗
PBS · ScriptsFrom 1 Oct 2026

Eligible nurse prescribers can now issue PBS scripts

From 1 October, eligible designated registered nurse prescribers can issue Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme (PBS) prescriptions, widening who can write the script you take to your pharmacy.

Source: Australian Pharmacist ↗
Allergy · BrisbaneSpring

Hayfever season: start treatment early

Brisbane's warm, humid climate keeps pollen and mould active for much of the year, with subtropical grasses common. Pharmacists suggest starting 1–2 weeks before your usual peak: a non-drowsy antihistamine such as fexofenadine is a common first step, sometimes combined with a corticosteroid nasal spray.
